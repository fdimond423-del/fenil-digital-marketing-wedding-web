Questions people actually ask

Is a digital wedding invitation acceptable in India?

Yes, and it has been normal for years. Most families now send the card on WhatsApp first and print a smaller run for elders and close relatives. A web invite does the job a printed card cannot: it carries the map, the timings, and a reply you can count.

Do guests need an app or an account to reply?

No. Each guest gets a private link. They open it in whatever browser their phone already has, tap yes or no per ceremony, and that is the whole thing. No install, no signup, no password.

Is a link safe to send? People are wary of wedding invitations now.

They are right to be, and the thing they are wary of is a file rather than a link. The widely-reported scam sends an attachment called something like “Wedding_Invitation.apk”, which is an app and not an invitation. A Toran invite is a web address that opens in the browser your guest already has: nothing downloads, nothing installs, and there is no file to tap. Sending the link with a line of your own text, rather than a bare URL, is most of what makes it read as genuine.

Can guests reply to some ceremonies and not others?

That is the main reason Toran exists. A guest can accept the Sangeet and decline the Tilak, and you see the count for each function separately rather than one number for the whole wedding.

Can I change the venue or time after sending the invite?

Yes, and that is the real advantage over a printed card. Edit it in the dashboard and anyone who opens the link afterwards sees the new details. There is nothing to resend.

Can I use this for a mundan, a griha pravesh or a naming day?

Yes, and it is the same account and the same price. Pick the occasion and the product follows it: a mundan is offered a havan, the mundan and the bhojan rather than a wedding’s ceremony list, the reply form stops asking which side of a couple a guest is on, and the preview that appears when the link is pasted into a family group describes a mundan rather than a wedding.

Can the invitation be in Hindi?

In Hindi, in English, or in both at once with a switch on the invitation itself, so each guest reads whichever they would rather. The Hindi is our own translation of every line the page shows a guest, reviewed line by line, and Devanagari is set in a face built for it rather than a Latin font pretending.

Can I use my own domain?

Not yet. Your invite lives on a toran address, which works exactly the same for guests, and is one fewer unfamiliar domain in a WhatsApp forward. If we add custom domains it will be to an existing site, not a reason to wait.

What does it cost?

₹1,999 once, which covers 24 months from the day you pay. Every template, unlimited guests and the full expense tracker are in it. Nothing renews on its own and no card is kept on file.

What happens after the 24 months are up?

The invite stays online for your guests. What stops is editing it: the dashboard asks you to add more time before it will let you change anything. Nothing is deleted, and adding months brings it all back.

Does it work for Muslim, Sikh and Christian weddings?

Yes. You pick a starting set of ceremonies, then rename, add and remove freely. Nothing assumes a Hindu ceremony list.

Can I track vendor payments too?

Yes, and it is half the product. Record what each vendor quoted, what you have paid in advance, what is still due and when, and tag spending to a specific ceremony. You then get a total for the sangeet rather than a total for décor, and a cost per head worked out from the guests who actually said yes to it.
