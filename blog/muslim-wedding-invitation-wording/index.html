Muslim wedding invitation wording for Nikah, Mehndi and Walima

A Muslim wedding invitation begins with the name of Allah and asks for the guests' presence and their du'as. Here is wording for a Muslim invitation card in English and Urdu, for every function.

A Nikah is a simple, sacred contract, but the celebrations around it can stretch over several days: the manjha or haldi, the Mehndi, the Nikah itself, the Rukhsati and the Walima hosted by the groom's family. A Muslim marriage card needs to communicate all of this with dignity, and it usually opens with Bismillah and closes by asking guests for their prayers.

Please note: the Urdu and Arabic samples below are kept short and simple. Families follow different customs, so please ask an elder, your local imam or a fluent Urdu reader to review your final wording.

The structure of a Muslim wedding invitation

  1. Opening: Bismillah, written in Arabic, in English translation or as "786" according to family preference.
  2. Hosts: usually the parents, often introduced with "Janab" and "Mohtarma" in Urdu or "Mr and Mrs" in English.
  3. The couple: the bride or groom of the hosting family, with the partner and their parents.
  4. The occasion: Nikah, Rukhsati or Walima.
  5. Date, time and venue, sometimes with the time of a prayer, such as "after Asr".
  6. Other functions: Mehndi, Manjha, Sangeet or Walima on separate lines.
  7. Closing: a line asking for du'as, followed by the family name.

Du'a and closing lines

That last line is the well-known du'a for newlyweds, meaning "May Allah bless you both, shower His blessings upon you and unite you in goodness." Many families include it in Arabic with an English translation underneath.

Etiquette for sacred words on a printed card

Some families prefer not to print Bismillah or Quranic verses in Arabic on paper that may later be thrown away. Common alternatives are writing "786", using an English translation, or placing sacred text only on a digital invitation. Whatever your family decides, keep sacred text at the top, never overlap it with images and avoid placing it near the RSVP or gift details.

Popular verses on wedding cards include Surah Ar-Rum 30:21, about Allah creating spouses so that you may find tranquillity in them and placing love and mercy between you. Quote it accurately from a trusted translation and ask your imam if you are unsure.

Frequently asked questions

What is written at the top of a Muslim wedding card?

Most cards open with Bismillah, either in Arabic (بسم اللہ الرحمٰن الرحیم), in English as "In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful", or as the number 786.

What is the difference between a Nikah and a Walima invitation?

The Nikah invitation is usually sent by the bride's family for the marriage ceremony. The Walima invitation is sent by the groom's family for the feast held after the Nikah.

Can I put a Quranic verse on a wedding invitation?

Many families do, often Surah Ar-Rum 30:21. Some prefer to avoid Arabic text on printed cards that may be discarded and use a translation or a digital invitation instead.

Should a Muslim marriage card be in Urdu or English?

Either, or both. Many families send an Urdu card to elders and an English version to friends and colleagues.
