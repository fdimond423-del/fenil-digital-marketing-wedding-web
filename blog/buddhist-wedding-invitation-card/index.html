Buddhist wedding invitation card wording and design ideas

Buddhist weddings in India are often simple and deeply meaningful, centred on vows, the Triple Gem and the blessings of family and monks. Here is how to word and design a Buddhist wedding invitation card.

Buddhism does not prescribe a single wedding ritual, so Buddhist weddings in India take many forms. Among Ambedkarite Buddhist families in Maharashtra and beyond, a wedding often includes reciting the Tisarana and Panchsila before images of the Buddha and Dr B. R. Ambedkar, followed by the couple's vows. In Ladakh, Sikkim and other Himalayan regions, weddings may involve monks, prayers and the exchange of khata scarves. Many families also blend these with their regional customs.

Please note: customs vary widely between communities. Treat the samples below as a starting point, and ask an elder, a bhante or a fluent speaker to review your wording.

The structure of a Buddhist wedding invitation

  1. A blessing: "Namo Buddhaya", or a line invoking the Triple Gem of the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha.
  2. Hosts: the parents or both families.
  3. The couple with their parents' names.
  4. The ceremony: date, time and venue, often a vihara, community hall or home.
  5. Other events: haldi, reception or a dhamma talk, as your family prefers.
  6. Closing: a line inviting guests to bless the couple.

Design ideas for a Buddhist wedding card

  • The lotus, a symbol of purity and awakening, perhaps the most fitting motif.
  • The Dhamma chakra (wheel of Dhamma), placed respectfully at the top.
  • The Bodhi leaf, simple and elegant.
  • Stupas and prayer flags for Himalayan Buddhist weddings.
  • Soft white, saffron, deep blue and gold for a calm palette.

Use images of the Buddha with care. Many families prefer symbols like the lotus or the wheel on invitations, keeping images of the Buddha for the ceremony itself, and they avoid placing sacred images where the card might be thrown away.

Etiquette notes for guests

If many of your guests have not attended a Buddhist wedding before, consider a short note on the card or the invitation website:

  • Dress modestly; white is common at many ceremonies.
  • Remove footwear before entering a vihara or shrine room.
  • Stay quiet during the recitation of the Tisarana and Panchsila.
  • Gifts are welcome but not expected; blessings matter most.

Frequently asked questions

What is written on a Buddhist wedding invitation?

A blessing such as "Namo Buddhaya" or a line invoking the Buddha, the Dhamma and the Sangha, the hosts, the couple and their parents, the ceremony details and a request for the guests' blessings.

What symbols are used on Buddhist wedding cards?

The lotus, the Dhamma chakra and the Bodhi leaf are common. Himalayan Buddhist weddings may use stupas, prayer flags or the eight auspicious symbols.

What happens at a Buddhist wedding in India?

It varies. Many Ambedkarite Buddhist weddings include reciting the Tisarana and Panchsila and the exchange of vows, while Himalayan Buddhist weddings may involve monks, prayers and khata scarves.

Can a Buddhist wedding invitation be in Marathi or Hindi?

Yes. Many families write the blessing and family lines in Marathi or Hindi and add English details for friends and colleagues.
