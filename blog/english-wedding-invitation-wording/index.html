English wedding invitation card wording for Indian weddings

An English wedding card has to work for grandparents, colleagues and friends from three different states. Here is marriage invitation wording in English that is clear, warm and correct.

English has become the common language of Indian wedding invitations, especially for families with guests across states and countries. But English wording has its own conventions: who "requests the honour", when to say "pleasure" and how to list several events without confusing anyone. This guide covers the main styles with samples you can adapt.

The parts of an English marriage invitation

  1. The hosts: traditionally the parents, sometimes the couple themselves.
  2. The request line: "request the honour of your presence" or "request the pleasure of your company".
  3. The couple: full names, often with the partner's parents.
  4. The event: date, day, time and venue.
  5. Additional events: on the same card or a separate insert.
  6. RSVP: a name, phone number or link, and a reply-by date.

"Honour of your presence" is traditionally used for religious ceremonies, while "pleasure of your company" suits receptions and less formal events. Either is acceptable today.

English etiquette notes for Indian cards

  • Write dates in words on formal cards and in numerals on modern ones. Do not mix both styles.
  • "Mrs and Mr" or "Mr and Mrs": both are used. Be consistent throughout.
  • Avoid "cordially invite you and family" if you mean only the person named. Address the envelope or message clearly instead.
  • Double-check the spelling of every name with the people involved.

Frequently asked questions

How do you write a marriage invitation in English?

Name the hosts, use a request line such as "request the honour of your presence", give the couple's names, then the date, time and venue, followed by any other events and RSVP details.

What is the difference between "honour of your presence" and "pleasure of your company"?

"Honour of your presence" is traditionally used for religious ceremonies; "pleasure of your company" is used for receptions and social events. Both are widely accepted today.

Should an Indian wedding card be in English or a regional language?

It depends on your guests. Many families use a regional language for blessings and family names and English for event details, so everyone can read the essentials.

Who should be named as the host on the card?

Traditionally the bride's parents host, but today both families or the couple themselves often host. Choose what reflects who is organising the wedding.
