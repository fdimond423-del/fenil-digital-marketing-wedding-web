Shadi card guide: what to write, how to design it and how to send it

Whether your family calls it a shadi card, sadi card or shaadi ka card, it is the first thing guests see of your wedding. Here is how to get the wording, design and timing right.

In most Indian homes, the shaadi ka card is a family project. One person handles the guest list, another argues about the colour and an elder decides whose name goes where. This guide brings all of it together so you can make decisions quickly and send your shadi invitation card on time.

What goes into a shaadi ka card

A traditional shadi card in North India usually follows this order. Families vary, so treat it as a checklist rather than a rule.

  1. An invocation or blessing at the top, such as "॥ श्री गणेशाय नमः ॥" for many Hindu families.
  2. The hosts: usually the bride's or groom's parents, sometimes grandparents.
  3. The invitation line asking guests to attend with family.
  4. The couple's names, often with their parents' names.
  5. The main ceremony: date, day, time (and muhurat, if your family follows it) and venue.
  6. Other functions: tilak, haldi, mehendi, sangeet, reception, each with its own date, time and venue.
  7. RSVP and contacts: one or two phone numbers for queries.
  8. A closing line from the family, such as "with best compliments from" followed by relatives' names, or a short note from the children of the family.

Choosing a design for your shadi card

The design should reflect your family and the wedding, not just the latest trend. A few popular directions:

  • Classic red and gold, with Ganesha, kalash or mandap motifs. Timeless for traditional families.
  • Royal Rajasthani, with jharokhas, palace arches and elephants. Works well for palace and haveli venues.
  • Floral pastel, with marigold, rose or jasmine illustrations. Popular for day weddings.
  • Minimal modern, with plenty of white space and a single motif. Good for city receptions.
  • Regional: phulkari embroidery for Punjabi weddings, kolam for Tamil weddings, alpana for Bengali weddings.

Whatever you choose, keep the event details easy to read. Fancy scripts look lovely but older guests need clear dates and times.

A simple shadi card timeline

  • 3 to 4 months before: finalise the guest list and the wording.
  • 10 to 12 weeks before: choose the design and get proofs.
  • 6 to 8 weeks before: distribute printed cards and send digital invitations.
  • 2 to 3 weeks before: send a reminder to outstation guests.
  • 2 days before each function: share the venue location again.

Common mistakes to avoid

  1. Spelling mistakes in names. Ask each side of the family to check their own names.
  2. Missing the day of the week next to the date.
  3. Writing "after 7 pm" when food will only be served at 9. Be honest about timings.
  4. Forgetting a phone number for directions on the day.
  5. Using a font so decorative that the venue name cannot be read.

Frequently asked questions

Is it "shadi card", "sadi card" or "shaadi ka card"?

All three are used for the same thing: a wedding invitation card. "Shaadi" is the closest spelling to the Hindi and Urdu word शादी, but people search and say it in many ways.

What should be written on a shadi card?

The hosts, the couple's names and parents' names, the main ceremony with date, time and venue, the other functions, a contact number and a closing line from the family. A blessing at the top is common in many communities.

When should we send the shaadi cards?

Six to eight weeks before the first function is standard. Send outstation and destination invitations earlier, ideally with a save-the-date a few months ahead.

Can a shadi card be sent on WhatsApp?

Yes. Digital shadi invitations are now widely accepted. Many families still hand-deliver printed cards to elders and close relatives, then send the digital invite to everyone else.
