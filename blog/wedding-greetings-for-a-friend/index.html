Wedding greetings for a friend, from heartfelt to hilarious

When it is your friend getting married, a generic message feels wrong. These wedding greetings are written for the people who knew them before the sherwani and the lehenga.

Friends have a special licence at weddings. You can be emotional, you can be funny, and you can remind the couple of the hostel days nobody else knows about. The trick is to strike the right balance for where your message will be read. A private WhatsApp chat can take a joke that a family group cannot.

How to write wedding greetings for a friend

  1. Start with the friendship. One line about how long you have known them or how you met.
  2. Say something true about the partner. Friends notice when you have made an effort to know their partner.
  3. Pick a single memory. The exam night, the first trip, the time they rehearsed the proposal on you.
  4. Finish with a wish for both of them. Bring it back to the couple, not just your friend.

Ideas for a friend's toast or speech

If you have been asked to speak at the sangeet or reception, take one of the heartfelt greetings above and build it into three short parts: a funny or warm story about your friend, a moment that showed you their partner was the one, and a toast to the couple. Keep it under three minutes and test it on someone who does not know your friend. If they laugh in the right places, you are ready.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good wedding greeting for a best friend?

Something personal: mention how long you have known them, one memory and a wish for both of them. For example, "From sharing notes in school to standing beside you today, it has been an honour. Congratulations, my friend."

Can I send a funny message to a friend on their wedding?

Yes, if you know they will enjoy it and it is going to them privately. Avoid jokes about exes, money or in-laws, and keep family groups for warmer messages.

How long should a wedding message for a friend be?

Two to four lines is ideal for a message. Save longer stories for a toast, a handwritten letter or a video message.

Should I wish my friend's partner too?

Always. Use both names and say something kind about the partner. It shows respect for the person your friend has chosen.
