Bengali wedding invitation card wording for your biyer card

A Bengali biyer card often opens with a small butterfly, the symbol of Prajapati, and speaks in a graceful, slightly old-fashioned Bengali. Here is how to write one, with samples you can adapt.

Few wedding cards are as recognisable as a Bengali one. The Prajapati butterfly, the alpana borders, the red and white of shankha and pola, and wording that often uses the formal sadhu bhasha style of Bengali, all make a biyer card feel like a small piece of heritage. Families frequently send two cards: one from the bride's side for the biye and one from the groom's side for the bou bhaat.

Please note: the Bengali samples below follow a simple, common style. Families in Kolkata, Bangladesh and elsewhere have their own customs, so ask an elder or a fluent Bengali speaker to review your final wording before you send it.

The structure of a Bengali biyer card

  1. Opening: "শ্রীশ্রী প্রজাপতয়ে নমঃ", invoking Prajapati, the deity of marriage. The butterfly motif comes from this.
  2. Salutation: "সবিনয় নিবেদন" (a respectful opening, like "With humble request").
  3. The announcement: the date, the couple and their parents.
  4. The request: asking guests to come and bless the couple.
  5. Programme: আশীর্বাদ, গায়ে হলুদ, বিবাহ, বৌভাত as relevant.
  6. Closing: the host's name, often with "বিনীত" (humbly).

The older sadhu bhasha forms like "হইবে" and "করিলে বাধিত হইব" are still loved on wedding cards. If your family prefers modern Bengali, use "হবে" and "করলে কৃতজ্ঞ থাকব" instead, and keep one style throughout.

Function lines

Bengali weddings often follow a লগ্ন (auspicious time) that can fall late at night. Mention it so guests can plan, and check every weekday against your wedding year.

Design ideas for a Bengali wedding card

  • Prajapati butterfly at the top, in red or gold.
  • Alpana patterns in white on red, inspired by floor art made with rice paste.
  • Shola craft motifs such as the topor, the groom's tall headdress, and the bride's mukut.
  • Shankha and pola bangles as a border.
  • Kantha stitch illustrations for a soft, handmade feel.

Menu cards and the feast

The feast is central to a Bengali wedding, and many families print a separate menu card for the biye or the bou bhaat. Our guide to the Bengali wedding menu card has menu wording in Bengali and English.

Printed card or digital invitation?

Printed biyer cards are still hand-delivered to elders, often with sweets. For friends, colleagues and relatives abroad, a digital invite is faster and lets you include both the biye and the bou bhaat with maps. Frequently asked questions

Why is there a butterfly on Bengali wedding cards?

The butterfly represents Prajapati, the deity associated with marriage. Cards often open with "শ্রীশ্রী প্রজাপতয়ে নমঃ" and a small butterfly motif.

What is a biyer card?

"Biyer card" (বিয়ের কার্ড) simply means wedding card in Bengali. It usually refers to the invitation from the bride's family for the wedding.

What is the difference between the biye card and the bou bhaat card?

The biye card is usually sent by the bride's family for the wedding. The bou bhaat card is sent by the groom's family for the reception held after the bride arrives at her new home.

Can a Bengali wedding card be in English?

Yes. Many families use Bengali for the opening and family lines and English for dates, times and venues, or send a separate English invitation to friends and colleagues.
