How to find wedding card printers near you

  • Search maps for "wedding card printers" and read recent reviews with photos of finished cards.
  • Visit your city's wedding card market. Many Indian cities have a lane or bazaar full of card shops; Chawri Bazar in Old Delhi is the best known.
  • Ask recently married friends and relatives who printed their cards and whether they would use them again.
  • Ask your wedding planner or decorator, who often work with printers.
  • Compare two or three printers before deciding.

Paper and finishes

  • GSM describes paper weight. Thicker paper feels more premium. Ask to touch samples rather than choosing from numbers.
  • Textured, handmade or metallic papers look beautiful but can affect how ink and photos print.
  • Matte or gloss: matte feels elegant; gloss makes photos pop.
  • Envelopes and boxes add cost and time. Confirm they are included.

Questions to ask a local printer

  1. Can I see and touch finished samples of this exact design and paper?
  2. What is the minimum order quantity?
  3. How many proof rounds are included, and will I get a physical proof?
  4. How long will printing take after I approve the proof?
  5. Are envelopes, inserts and boxes included in the price?
  6. Do you have good fonts for Hindi, Marathi, Tamil or my language?
  7. Who checks the regional language text, and who is responsible for errors?
  8. Can you deliver, or will I need to collect?
  9. What payment do you need upfront, and when is the balance due?
  10. Can I get the final design file for my records?

Get the quote, timeline and inclusions in writing, even if only on WhatsApp.

Approving the proof

The proof is your last chance to catch mistakes. Check names, dates, weekdays, times, venues, phone numbers and regional script with two people from different sides of the family. Our wedding card editing guide has a full checklist. Ask for a physical proof if colours matter; screens and paper show colours differently.

A realistic printing timeline

  • 12 weeks before the first function: choose a printer and design.
  • 10 weeks before: finalise wording and approve proofs.
  • 8 weeks before: printing and finishing.
  • 6 to 8 weeks before: collect and distribute cards.

Add extra time for boxes, laser cutting or busy wedding season months.

What changes the price of printed cards

We will not quote numbers, because prices vary a lot by city, season and printer. These are the factors that move the price up or down:

  • Quantity: larger runs usually cost less per card.
  • Number of inserts: each extra card adds printing and handling.
  • Paper: handmade, metallic and heavy papers cost more.
  • Finishes: foil, embossing, laser cutting and letterpress add cost and time.
  • Boxes and packaging: often the most expensive part of a premium invite.
  • Urgency: rush jobs in peak season may cost more.
  • Corrections: changes after the proof is approved can mean a reprint.

Ask each printer to quote the same specification, so you are comparing like with like. A cheaper quote that leaves out envelopes, inserts or delivery often ends up costing more once everything is added.

How long does wedding card printing take?

Usually a few weeks including design, proofs and printing, and longer for boxes, laser cutting or during peak wedding season. Start about 12 weeks before your first function.
