Gujarati wedding invitation card wording for your kankotri

In Gujarati homes the wedding card is called the kankotri, and the first one is traditionally offered to the family deity. Here is how a Gujarati invitation card is written, with sample wording in Gujarati script.

A Gujarati wedding is a string of joyful events: Ganesh sthapana, mandap muhurat, pithi, mameru, garba and finally the wedding with its hast melap. The kankotri has to hold all of that, and it usually does so with warmth, a little formality and a long list of welcoming relatives at the end.

A note before you copy: the Gujarati samples here use simple, common wording. Communities and families have their own customs, so please ask an elder or a fluent Gujarati speaker to check your final text and the muhurat.

The structure of a Gujarati kankotri

  1. Invocation: "॥ શ્રી ગણેશાય નમઃ ॥" and often the name of the family deity (કુળદેવી).
  2. Hosts: the parents, sometimes with grandparents.
  3. The couple: the bride or groom of the family, then the partner with their parents' names.
  4. The invitation to attend with family and bless the couple.
  5. Programme: each function with date, time and venue.
  6. Closing: the family names under a line such as "નિમંત્રક".

Gujarati wedding functions to list

You do not need to list every ritual. Include the events where you expect guests, and keep family-only rituals for a separate message.

Function lines in Gujarati

Gujarati numerals (૧, ૨, ૩) look beautiful on a card, but plain numerals are easier for many guests. Choose one style and use it everywhere, and check the weekday against your wedding year.

Design ideas for a Gujarati wedding card

  • Bandhani patterns in red, yellow and green.
  • Ganesha with a kalash and toran, the classic opening motif.
  • Garba dancers and dandiya sticks for the raas invitation.
  • Patola-inspired borders for an elegant, heritage feel.
  • Mirror-work colours: bright, festive and very Gujarati.

The first kankotri

Many families place the first kankotri before the family deity or at the temple, and send the second to the maternal uncle's family. If you are going digital, you can still keep this ritual with a single printed card, then send the digital invitation to everyone else.

Printed or digital kankotri?

A printed kankotri is wonderful for elders and close relatives. For the rest of a large Gujarati guest list, a wedding invitation website is more practical: each function gets its own card with a map, guests can reply on WhatsApp, and late changes such as a new garba venue update on the same link. Frequently asked questions

What is a Gujarati wedding card called?

It is called a કંકોત્રી (kankotri). The word is used for the wedding invitation card in Gujarati homes.

What should a Gujarati marriage invitation card include?

A blessing such as "શ્રી ગણેશાય નમઃ", the hosts, the couple's and parents' names, the main functions with dates, times and venues, and a closing line with the family names.

Which functions are usually printed on a Gujarati wedding card?

Commonly the mandap muhurat, garba or dandiya raas, the wedding and the reception. Family-only rituals like the mameru are often shared separately.

Can I make a Gujarati invitation card in English?

Yes. Many families combine a Gujarati blessing with English event details, or send an English version to friends and colleagues.
